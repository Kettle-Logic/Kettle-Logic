# Ingredients

This page shows you how to add malt, hops, yeast, and other ingredients to a recipe.

## Add an ingredient

1. Open the recipe, and then click the tab for the ingredient: **Fermentables**, **Hops**, **Yeast**, **Water**, or **Miscellaneous**.
2. Click **Add** in that section.
3. Click **Choose…** beside **Common ingredients**, find the ingredient, and click it. Or enter its details yourself.
4. Enter the amount, and then confirm.

To find an ingredient in a long list:

- **Search.** Type part of a name. Case, accents, and punctuation do not matter, so `2 row` finds `2-Row (Briess)`. Press the Down and Up arrow keys to move through the results, and press Enter to pick the highlighted one.
- **Sort.** Pick an order from **Catalog order**, **Name**, **Group**, or a figure such as color or alpha acid, and whether it runs **Ascending** or **Descending**.
- **Filter.** Click **Filter** to show only some groups, types, or forms, or your own ingredients. Type in the **from** and **to** fields to limit a figure, such as color, to a range. Each active filter shows as a button you can click to remove it, and **Clear filters** removes them all.

Each ingredient shows its key figures under its name, so you can compare them without leaving the list.

To change an ingredient, click its name. To remove it, click the **Remove** button beside it, or open its menu and click **Remove**.

## Set hop timing

For each hop, pick **When to add**. Then enter the time, in minutes, for a hop added during the boil.

## Make a custom ingredient

1. Click **Ingredient Directory** in the sidebar.
2. Click **New Custom Ingredient**, and then pick the kind of ingredient.
3. Enter the name and the values from the package or the supplier, and then save it.

Your custom ingredients appear under **Your Ingredients**, and you can add them to any recipe.

## Enter the values from the package

- **Alpha acid (%)** for hops: the bittering strength, printed on the package.
- **Attenuation (%)** for yeast: how much sugar the yeast ferments. The packet lists a typical figure.

Kettle Logic uses these values in its calculations, so enter the figures from your own packages.
